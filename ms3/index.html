Does not transport for clinical deployment. Discrimination is essentially equivalent to biopsy ISUP plus log-PSA alone. The successor study (MS4) reframes the major-upgrade question from continuous-risk prediction to high-risk subgroup identification within biopsy GG1-2 patients, on a composite adverse-pathology endpoint (RP GG ≥ 3, EPE, SVI, or pN+).

Predictors and pre-specified transformations

Seven predictors: age, log(PSA + 1), positive-core ratio, fusion-biopsy status, clinical T stage (T1 / T2 / T3+), PI-RADS category (1-2 / 3 / 4 / 5), biopsy ISUP (1 / 2 / 3 / 4 for any-upgrade; 1 / 2 only for major-upgrade subset). Categorical references: cT1, PI-RADS 1-2, ISUP 1.

Validation summary

Polish derivation cohort: single-surgeon, two-centre series, n = 820 with biopsy ISUP 1-4 (any-upgrade) and n = 616 with biopsy ISUP 1-2 (major-upgrade subset). US validation cohort: BWH multi-surgeon academic series, n = 595 (any-upgrade) and n = 378 (major-upgrade subset). Cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals on US-cohort performance metrics; CR2-corrected cluster-robust standard errors on coefficient inference. Reciprocal validation (US-derive, Polish-validate) returned external AUC 0.78 with calibration slope 0.60 and intercept -0.38, mirroring the discrimination-transports-but-calibration-drifts pattern in the opposite direction. Decision-curve analysis on the recalibrated any-upgrade model exceeded both the treat-all and treat-none reference strategies across the pre-committed 10-40% threshold range.

Research tool — not for clinical decision-making. Predicts ISUP grade-group upgrading at radical prostatectomy in patients already selected for surgery. Both cohorts are RP-only, which introduces verification bias: men with biopsy ISUP 1 who remain on active surveillance never enter the dataset. This tool must not be used for active-surveillance eligibility decisions. The any-upgrade model is reasonable for pre-RARP counselling after local intercept updating among men already selected for surgery; the major-upgrade specification is reported for transparency and is research-only. Predictions do not replace clinical judgment.
