ISUP Upgrade Risk: Active-Surveillance Counselling Tool

This tool estimates the chance of finding a higher prostatectomy grade than the biopsy showed if a patient undergoes surgery. It is designed to support shared decisions for patients with biopsy ISUP grade 1 or 2 who are considering active surveillance.

Jobczyk M, Clinton TN, De Carvalho FL, Ciechanowski P, Mouw KW, Fendler W, Stawiski K. Externally validated biopsy-to-prostatectomy ISUP upgrade risk model: a TRIPOD+AI report supporting active-surveillance counselling. Submitted to Prostate Cancer and Prostatic Diseases, 2026. MS3 v1.0 (2026-05-23).

How to use this tool. Enter the biopsy results and clinical details. The calculator provides the calibrated chance of finding a higher prostatectomy grade than the biopsy showed, along with a rule-in check at the 0.65 threshold (predicted probability 0.65). In the external validation group of 357 active-surveillance-eligible patients (biopsy ISUP grade 1 or 2, n=357), this 0.65 threshold identified a group where about 85 in 100 patients had a higher grade at surgery (positive predictive value 85.5%, 95% CI 71.2-93.4). Use the rule-in check to identify patients who may need closer discussion about surgery, and use the calibrated probability to guide surveillance decisions for other patients.
Predicting a major upgrade in grade is research-only. This tool worked less well in independent testing for this endpoint, returning an area under the curve of 0.635 (95% confidence interval 0.501 to 0.729). The lower bound is close to chance, and the slope was less reliable at 62 events. The calculator displays this probability for transparency only. Do not use this endpoint to make active surveillance decisions or to plan surgery templates. The model applies only to patients with biopsy grade 1 or 2, and it requires all 7 patient details.

Predictors and pre-specified transformations

Full specification has seven predictors: age (years), log(PSA + 1) (ng/mL), positive-core ratio, MRI-fusion biopsy status, clinical T stage (T1 / T2 / T3+), PI-RADS category (1-2 / 3 / 4 / 5), and biopsy ISUP (1 / 2 / 3 / 4 because ISUP 5 is excluded as upgrade is mathematically impossible). Categorical reference levels: cT1, PI-RADS 1-2, ISUP 1. The parsimonious 2-predictor specification uses only biopsy ISUP and log(PSA + 1).

Cohort summary

Polish derivation cohort: consecutive single-surgeon prostatectomies at SalveMedica and Bełchatów Hospital between January 2017 and December 2024. n = 820 with biopsy ISUP 1-4 for the any-upgrade endpoint. US validation cohort: consecutive multi-surgeon prostatectomies at Brigham and Women's Hospital between January 2018 and December 2024. n = 562 for the any-upgrade endpoint, and biopsy ISUP 1-2 AS-eligible subset n = 357. Reciprocal-direction validation (US-derive then Polish-validate) returned external AUC 0.781 (95% CI 0.748-0.814) with calibration slope 0.626 drift, confirming symmetric discrimination transport with calibration drift that supports site-specific recalibration.

Why this calculator is designed for AS-entry counselling

The biopsy-to-prostatectomy ISUP comparison is the exact metric for active-surveillance-entry counselling, because it isolates sampling-error reclassification from biological progression. The AS-eligible biopsy ISUP 1-2 subset is the population for whom AS is clinically considered. External validation in that subset returned AUC 0.771 with PPV 85.5% at the Polish-derived rule-in operating point. Both cohorts are prostatectomy-conditioned, introducing verification bias. Baseline-risk recalibration to a true AS cohort is required before deployment. We therefore present this calculator as a research-grade decision aid for upgrade-risk communication, not as an AS-eligibility triage tool.

Research tool for shared decision-making, not for clinical decisions. The model predicts a higher prostatectomy grade than the biopsy showed in patients who underwent surgery. Both the development and validation groups are restricted to patients who chose surgery, which introduces verification bias against patients who remain on active surveillance. The calibrated probability helps inform counselling, and the threshold classification is a rule-in aid rather than a definitive decision tool. Predictions do not replace clinical judgement, biopsy or imaging reviews, or patient preferences. This tool is not CE-marked or FDA-approved.
